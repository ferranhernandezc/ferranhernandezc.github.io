Ferran Hernandez Caralt

PhD Student at the University of Cambridge

I am Ferran Hernandez Caralt, a PhD Candidate at the University of Cambridge, supervised by Prof. Pietro Liò. With a background in mathematics and experience in the fundamentals of machine learning, my research focuses on the foundations and limitations of graph neural networks and their higher-order variants, specifically when applied to graph-structured data.

My academic journey started with the CFIS scholarship, which allowed me to pursue a double degree in Mathematics and Data Science & Engineering at the Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya. I developed my Bachelor’s thesis at the University of Cambridge under the supervision of Prof. Pietro Liò on Sheaf Neural Networks.

Afterwards, I spent time in industry gaining hands-on R&D and engineering experience at Qualcomm as an AI Research Engineering intern and then at Axiomatic AI as an AI engineer building agentic systems for photonic integrated circuit design.

Supported by a La Caixa Postgraduate Fellowship Abroad, I returned to Cambridge for my PhD.

  4. Joint Diffusion Processes as an Inductive Bias in Sheaf Neural Networks
    Ferran Hernandez Caralt, Guillermo Bernárdez Gil, Iulia Duta, and 2 more authors
    In Proceedings of the Geometry-grounded Representation Learning and Generative Modeling Workshop (GRaM), 29 jul 2024
    Abs PDF

    Sheaf Neural Networks (SNNs) naturally extend Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) by endowing a cellular sheaf over the graph, equipping nodes and edges with vector spaces and defining linear mappings between them. While the attached geometric structure has proven to be useful in analyzing heterophily and oversmoothing, so far the methods by which the sheaf is computed do not always guarantee a good performance in such settings. In this work, drawing inspiration from opinion dynamics concepts, we propose two novel sheaf learning approaches that (i) provide a more intuitive understanding of the involved structure maps, (ii) introduce a useful inductive bias for heterophily and oversmoothing, and (iii) infer the sheaf in a way that does not scale with the number of features, thus using fewer learnable parameters than existing methods. In our evaluation, we show the limitations of the real-world benchmarks used so far on SNNs, and design a new synthetic task –leveraging the symmetries of n-dimensional ellipsoids– that enables us to better assess the strengths and weaknesses of sheaf-based models. Our extensive experimentation on these novel datasets reveals valuable insights into the scenarios and contexts where basic SNNs and our proposed approaches can be beneficial.
